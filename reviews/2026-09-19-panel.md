# Panel review of Program Charter v0.1

Date: 2026-09-19. Three reviewers, each a role played by an AI agent with no knowledge of the design conversation. They are not real people and their views are not anyone's actual opinion. Each read `README.md` and `charter.spec.yaml` at v0.1 cold and was asked where the format is naive or inefficient.

| Seat | Perspective |
|---|---|
| A | former DARPA program manager |
| B | senior program person at a foresight and field-building institute |
| C | operator of small private research programs (3 to 8 people, fixed term, raises from philanthropic funders and research-minded investors) |

## Where all three agreed

1. **The derivation rule was backwards.** "Nothing appears lower down that cannot be traced upward" assumes research flows top-down. Real programs learn from below, the best result is often unplanned, and a strict rule gets back-filled. B noted it contradicted the format's own sketch, where discovery work came before the thesis. C noted the program had no legitimate place for unplanned work.
2. **Layer 3 should be called "open problems".** Outsiders search for and cite that phrase. "Agenda" sounds like a meeting and blurs into "roadmap".
3. **Too much apparatus for a small team.** Five update cadences, eight document types, a separate space object, a five-stage lifecycle. Cut the cadences, cut the document registry, keep the space inside the charter until a second program exists, write deeper documents only on demand.
4. **Ending was named but not conditioned.** "Graduate, hand off, or stop" lists outcomes. It needs the condition that triggers each.
5. **The "call" step assumes money to hand out.** Optional at best for an internal group.

## Where two agreed

- **Naming a territory does not plant a flag** (A, B). People adopt a space for results in it or for a shared asset: an arena, a benchmark, a dataset, a convening, money.
- **Judgment criteria were missing from the top level** (A, C). Success at end, baseline, kill or drop conditions, dated go/no-go, all written before work starts.
- **The terms belong last in the body with the key items surfaced on page one** (B, C). A wanted them first as a half-page block.

## Where they split

- **The competitor test.** A: keep as a sorting heuristic only, since the beliefs that define a space are usually the contested ones. B: it selects for platitudes; add contested beliefs. C: good, do not touch.
- **The name.** A: Program Charter. B: Research Program Charter, and call it a format until a second organisation uses it. C: Program Brief, reserving "charter" for the terms.

## Single-reviewer points taken

- A: sponsor and decision authority, a transition customer, people's time fractions as the real budget, risks and dependencies, an openness rule, reporting.
- B: the tree belongs with the open problems as the public map, with a named maintainer, and the roadmap is the path through it; stewardship after the term; what is on offer to outsiders; per-question status, prior work, skill to start, who is on it, contact; stable ids never renumbered; a hazard flag; a contribution path.
- C: the one-pager is primary and the charter is its appendix; a minimum version of two to three pages in about two person-days; a 90-day test of the format itself; about 20% of the envelope reserved for untraced work.

## Decisions in v0.2

| Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Name | **Program Charter**, described as a format | two of three kept "charter"; the author prefers it; shortest option; B's humility about "standard" adopted |
| Layer 3 | **Open problems** | unanimous; overrides the earlier "agenda" |
| Terms first or last | **Last in the body, with a masthead box on page one** | B and C; satisfies A's point that the binding part must be seen first |
| Derivation rule | a check at review, not a gate; untraced work allowed, labelled, budgeted, and forced to resolve after two reviews; a falsified premise forces a new version | unanimous |
| Competitor test | kept as a sorting heuristic; **cruxes** added to the space; the thesis states our side of each crux | reconciles A, B and C |
| Flag | dropped as a claim; **shared_asset** added to the space | A and B |
| Tree | moved into open problems; roadmap holds the path | B |
| Cadences, voice column, document registry, separate space object | cut from the spec; "writes as" kept as a one-line hint per part | A, B, C |
| Judgment criteria | success_at_end, baseline, drop_condition per premise, go_no_go, transition now required | A, C |
| Size | week-one version 2 to 3 pages; full charter 6 pages at most | A, C |
| Format test | added, with a failure sign: "a slide deck has become the real plan" | C |

## Not taken

- C's "Program Brief". A brief does not constitute anything, and the terms are what make this more than a plan.
- A's "cut either the YAML or the README". Kept both, but the README is now short and the YAML is the single source; the README embeds it and restates nothing.
- A fixed page cap on the open problems list. Left to the program.
